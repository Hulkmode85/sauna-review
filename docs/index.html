How Does Infrared Heat Work?

Traditional saunas heat the surrounding air, which then heats your body from the outside in. Infrared saunas work differently. Far-infrared wavelengths (roughly 5.6-1000 micrometers) penetrate up to 1.5 inches into your skin, heating your body directly at the cellular level.

This is why infrared saunas operate at lower ambient temperatures (120-150°F vs. 180-220°F for Finnish saunas) while still producing profuse sweating. Your core body temperature rises, triggering the same cardiovascular and detoxification responses as intense exercise.

The HigherDOSE blanket specifically uses a multi-layer system:

  • Far-infrared layer — the primary heat source that penetrates deep tissue
  • Tourmaline layer — a semi-precious mineral that generates negative ions when heated, which may improve mood and reduce oxidative stress
  • Charcoal layer — binds to and neutralizes odors while providing even heat distribution
  • Amethyst layer — used in traditional healing; adds another layer of infrared-emitting crystals

Proven Benefits of Infrared Sauna Use

Peer-reviewed research supports a number of health benefits from regular infrared sauna use:

1. Cardiovascular Health

A 2018 study in BMC Medicine followed 2,315 men for 20 years and found that those who used a sauna 4-7 times per week had a 63% lower risk of sudden cardiac death compared to once-per-week users. Infrared sauna use elevates heart rate to 100-150 BPM, mimicking moderate cardio exercise.

2. Deep Detoxification

Infrared-induced sweat contains up to 20% toxins (heavy metals, BPA, phthalates) compared to only 3% in exercise-induced sweat. A 2012 study in the Journal of Environmental and Public Health found that sweating mobilizes stored heavy metals including arsenic, cadmium, lead, and mercury.

3. Pain Relief and Recovery

NASA research demonstrated that far-infrared light increases tissue growth and cell regeneration. For athletes, this translates to faster muscle recovery, reduced delayed onset muscle soreness (DOMS), and decreased joint stiffness. Many physical therapists now recommend infrared therapy for chronic pain conditions.

4. Improved Sleep

The rapid body temperature rise followed by a natural cool-down mimics your circadian temperature drop before sleep. Users consistently report deeper sleep on sauna nights. A Japanese study found that infrared therapy improved sleep quality in participants with chronic fatigue syndrome.

5. Skin Health and Anti-Aging

Increased blood flow delivers more oxygen and nutrients to skin cells. A German study found that regular infrared sauna use improved skin texture, reduced wrinkles, and increased collagen production over 12 weeks.

6. Stress and Mood

Sauna use triggers the release of endorphins, norepinephrine, and dynorphins. The heat stress also activates heat shock proteins (HSPs), which protect cells from damage and have been linked to longevity in multiple studies.
